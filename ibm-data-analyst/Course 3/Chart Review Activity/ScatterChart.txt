A scatter chart (scatter plot) is a graph that displays the relationship between two numerical variables. Each point on the chart represents one observation, with one value plotted on the X-axis and the other on the Y-axis. It helps identify patterns, trends, clusters, and possible correlations.

Use Case -> A hospital wants to know if there's a relationship between a patient's age and their recovery time after surgery.

Advantages -> 
Easy to see if two things go up/down together
Shows every single data point, nothing is hidden or averaged
Easy to spot the "odd one out" (like one super old coaster that's really fast)
Works fine even with lots of data points

Disadvantages -> 
Only works with numbers (can't use it for things like names or categories)
Too many dots close together can look messy and hard to read
Just because two things look related on the chart doesn't mean one causes the other
Harder for people who aren't used to reading charts to understand at a glance

Why to use scatter chart for this example?
You have 50 roller coasters, each with two numeric values: Year Opened (1932–2007) and Speed (26.8–80 mph).
Scatter charts are specifically designed for exactly this — two numeric variables where you want to see if there's a relationship (e.g., "do newer coasters tend to be faster?").
50 data points is a good amount — enough to spot a pattern, but not so many that the chart gets cluttered/unreadable.
